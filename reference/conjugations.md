## Notes on irregular forms

- **أَحَبَّ (aHabba, "to love, to like")**: doubled (geminate) root ح-ب-ب. The feminine plural forms unfold the doubled consonant: تُحْبِبْنَ (tuHbibna) and يُحْبِبْنَ (yuHbibna), not the تُحِبِّنَ pattern the rest of the paradigm would suggest.
- **أَرَادَ (araada, "to want")**: hollow root. The long ي shortens in the feminine plural: تُرِدْنَ (turidna) and يُرِدْنَ (yuridna).
- **أَكَلَ (akala, "to eat")**: hamza-initial root. The "I" form is آكُلُ (aakulu): the prefix hamza and the root's own initial hamza merge into an alif madda.
- **قَرَأَ (qara'a, "to read")**: hamza-final root. The hamza stays seated on the line even before the ونَ plural ending (تَقْرَأُونَ). The dual uses alif madda (تَقْرَآنِ).
- **سَكَنَ (sakana, "to live")**: sound root, but its final radical ن collides with the feminine plural suffix نَ and the two merge with a shadda: تَسْكُنَّ (taskunna), يَسْكُنَّ (yaskunna).
- **تَرْجَمَ (tarjama, "to translate")**: a quadriliteral verb (four root consonants, ت-ر-ج-م), not a triliteral Form II. It conjugates the same way Form II does (Damma prefix, kasra before the final radical), just with an extra consonant: يُتَرْجِمُ.
- All twelve Al-Kitaab verbs (Forms I, II, III, IV, and V, plus the one quadriliteral above) are otherwise fully regular within their form's own template. See the Verb Forms (al-Awzaan) lesson for how the ten forms relate to each other.
